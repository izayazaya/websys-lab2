Day 1: The Homelabbing Ritual

Tearing Down to Build it Better

There’s a unique kind of therapy in a terminal window. Spent part of my day doing maintenance on my home server setup. Sometimes it feels like I’m fixing things that aren’t broken just to see if I can optimize a config file or shave a few megabytes off idle memory usage. Total Free and Open Source Software (FOSS) purist behavior? Maybe. But watching services spin up flawlessly under Debian makes the occasional headache entirely worth it.

Day 2: Clear Skies and Dusty Roads

Outrunning the Code

Stepped away from the screens today to clear my head with a run around town. Living in Albay means the views are great, but the heat is no joke, so you have to time it right. Running is one of those few times my brain actually stops compiling problems and just focuses on the next step. No logic loops, no debugging, just pace and breathing. Hit my target distance and headed back before the sun completely melted me.

Day 3: The Linux Daily Driver Dream

The Career Compass

Was reflecting a bit today on what comes next after university. Looking back at stuff like the IT Olympics last year, it’s clear where my comfort zone is. My ultimate career goal is deceptively simple: find a role that pays well enough to fund my hobbies, and crucially, lets me use Linux as my daily driver without corporate forcing Windows down my throat. If I can spend my 9-to-5 in a bash shell and get compensated fairly for it, I’ve won.

Day 4: Low Social Battery, High Technical Focus

The Comfort of Hard Logic

Some days, the mental bandwidth for dealing with people is just sitting at absolute zero. Socializing takes an amount of processing power I didn’t have to spare today. Instead, I channeled that energy into some programming and electronics work. Electronics are comforting because they follow strict physical and logical rules, if a circuit fails, or code throws an exception, there is always a rational reason why. You can't say the same about human interactions.
